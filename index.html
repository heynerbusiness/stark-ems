02

Why EMS

The short, honest science — no miracle claims, just a smarter signal.

EMS — electrical muscle stimulation — sends low-frequency impulses to your muscles through electrodes in a fitted suit.

Those impulses mimic the signals your own brain sends. The difference: they reach more muscle fibers, deeper layers, all at the same time — including the deep stabilizing muscles along your spine that conventional training barely reaches. You perform simple, guided movements; the impulse makes every contraction count.

Because the work comes from the contraction itself, there is no need to load your joints with heavy weights. Intensity goes up; joint stress stays down. That is what makes EMS genuinely low-impact — and why one focused 20-minute session, once a week, is a complete program rather than a warm-up.

Straight talk: EMS is a fitness and strength service — not a medical treatment, and not a shortcut around consistency. It works when you show up weekly. We will never promise you anything else.

Let's be clear: EMS doesn't replace full training, and it doesn't replace gym training. But if you can't do gym training because you have no time, and you use 20 minutes instead of 2–3 hours, then it's very good for you: in very little time, a whole lot gets activated.

Questions, answered

What exactly is EMS?+

EMS stands for electrical muscle stimulation. You wear a fitted suit with electrodes over all major muscle groups. During simple guided movements, low-frequency impulses make your muscles contract — deeper and more completely than voluntary training alone. Your trainer controls everything, 1-on-1, for 20 minutes.

Does it hurt?+

No. It feels like a strong tingling pressure while your muscles work — unusual the first time, then simply intense. Most clients describe it as intense but comfortable. You and your trainer set the intensity together, per muscle group, and you can turn it down at any moment.

How often should I train?+

Once a week. That is the program: one focused 20-minute session per week, every week. EMS is a full-body stimulus, so your muscles need the days between to recover and adapt — that recovery is where the progress happens.

When will I see results?+

That depends on your starting point, your goal and — above all — consistency, which is why we don't guess. We measure: your strength and body composition are checked at the start and re-checked every 6 weeks, so progress shows up in numbers, not in promises. What we can promise is the structure: one focused session a week, every week.

What do I wear or bring?+

Almost nothing. We bring the suit and your personal undergarment set (included with packs, yours to keep). You need about 2 × 2 meters of space, a water bottle, and 30 minutes of your day. No shoes, no gym bag, no equipment.

Is it safe?+

We train exclusively on FDA-cleared devices, with 1-on-1 supervision and a short health check before your first session. EMS is not for everyone: it is off-limits with pacemakers or electronic implants, during pregnancy, with epilepsy or acute illness. See the Safety section for the full list — we publish it because we mean it.
